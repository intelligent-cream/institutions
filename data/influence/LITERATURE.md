# What is already known

A scan of prior work (October 2026). [V] = confirmed in the source; [M] = from memory, not re-checked.

## Lobbying and tax (US only)

- Richter, Samphantharak & Timmons (2009), *Lobbying and Taxes*, AJPS 53(4) [V]: higher LDA lobbying spend goes with lower effective tax rates the following year.
- Meade & Li (2015), *Strategic corporate tax lobbying*, JATA 37(2) [V]: only "strategic" tax lobbying by high-ETR firms predicts lower future cash ETRs.
- Chen, Dyreng & Li, *Corporate Political Contributions and Tax Avoidance*, SSRN 2403918 [V, finding not checked]: PAC giving and tax avoidance.
- Kim & Zhang (2016), Contemporary Accounting Research [V]: politically connected firms are more tax aggressive.
- Alexander, Mazza & Scholz (2009) [V]: more than $220 returned per $1 lobbied for the 2004 repatriation holiday.
- Barrick, Brown & Alexander, review of 29 studies on tax-related political activity [V]: SSRN 2841803.

## Lobbying and government contracts (US only)

- Igan et al. (IMF WP 2019; JEBO 2025) [V]: firms that kept lobbying through the 2013 sequestration won more contracts afterwards.
- Ağca & Igan (BIS WP 1058, 2022) [V]: lobbying firms received about a third more defence contracts (non-causal).

## Same firms, US and EU

- Hanegraaff, Poletti & Yildirim (2026), Interest Groups & Advocacy 15(2) [V]: firm-centric trade lobbying in the US and EU, 2014–2018, at sector and firm level; trade only.
- Chalmers & Macedo (2021), JEPP 28(12) [V]: EU lobbying spend and firm profits, 728 firms, EU only.
- Kim & Milner, LobbyView (MIT) [V]: all US public firms' lobbying 1999–2019, US only.
- Borderless Politics dataset [V]: 2,043 firms' participation across national, EU and UN venues, 1995–2016.

## Descriptive NGO work

- Oxfam *Rigged Reform* (2017) and US PIRG / Citizens for Tax Justice *Dirty Thirty* (2011–12) [V]: US lobbying spend next to taxes paid and tax-haven subsidiaries (from SEC Exhibit 21), US firms only.
- ITEP *Offshore Shell Games* [V]: tax-haven subsidiaries of the Fortune 500, no lobbying.
- GAO-09-157 [V]: large federal contractors with tax-haven subsidiaries, no lobbying.

## Tax-haven subsidiaries from GLEIF

- No paper found that counts tax-haven subsidiaries from GLEIF Level 2 data. Existing work uses SEC Exhibit 21 or Orbis. The FSB (2019) notes many subsidiaries of large firms still lack an LEI [V].

## What this project adds

1. One panel per firm and year joining US lobbying (LDA), PAC giving (FEC), EU lobbying and Commission meetings, German and French registers, effective tax rates, US contracts and tax-haven subsidiaries. No prior study found joins more than two of these across both sides of the Atlantic.
2. Effective tax rates of European firms from their own ESEF filings, alongside SEC data for US filers.
3. An open, reproducible count of tax-haven subsidiaries from GLEIF (to be validated against Exhibit 21).
4. The questions with least prior work: whether the same firm shifts lobbying between Washington and Brussels; whether EU and national lobbying relate to European firms' tax rates; total cross-border influence spend against tax-haven presence.

With about 110 firms, results are descriptive, not causal.
